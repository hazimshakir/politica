The seven dimensions of political thinking

Most people hold political views that don’t fit neatly on one line. POLITICA’s political values assessment looks at seven separate dimensions so that mixed and uncertain views count as real answers.

Begin assessment  →

Why one line isn’t enough

A single left-to-right line squeezes very different questions into one position. Two people at the same point can disagree about much of what matters to them, and one person can hold views that pull in different directions. A one-line political spectrum can’t show that.

Looking at several dimensions separately lets you see where your views are consistent, where they are mixed, and where you are simply unsure. None of those is a mistake.

The seven dimensions

Each dimension is a question people answer in different ways. POLITICA doesn’t treat any answer as the right one.

  1. 01 / Economy

    Economic philosophy

    How much should markets, taxation and public provision shape economic life? People weigh efficiency, fairness, security and opportunity differently, and many hold mixed views, such as valuing open markets alongside a strong safety net.

  2. 02 / Society

    Social philosophy

    How should a society balance individual freedom, equality and shared duties? This dimension is about what people owe one another and how far personal choices should be left to individuals.

  3. 03 / Culture

    Cultural outlook

    How do you view tradition and change? Some people place high value on continuity, heritage and established norms; others on openness and reform. Many value both, in different areas of life.

  4. 04 / Democracy

    Democratic values

    How should power be shared and held to account? It covers views on dissent, institutions, participation, and how decisions should be made and challenged.

  5. 05 / Governance

    Government & liberty

    How much power should the state have, and where should individual rights set the limits? It covers the balance between security and freedom, and between rules and discretion.

  6. 06 / World

    National & global outlook

    How do you weigh national identity and self-government against international cooperation? It covers borders, trade and how to approach problems that cross them.

  7. 07 / Values

    Personal principles

    Which principles shape your sense of fairness: merit, justice, responsibility, compassion? This dimension looks at the values that sit beneath specific policy positions.

How the dimensions fit together

The dimensions are kept apart because they can come apart. Someone may be cautious about change in culture and open to it in economics, or value national identity and international cooperation at once. In the assessment, mixed answers like these are treated as meaningful, not as inconsistency.

What this framework is, and isn’t

POLITICA is an experimental, non-validated tool for self-reflection. The seven dimensions are a practical framework chosen for that purpose, not a standard or definitive classification of political thought, and other frameworks divide the ground differently. Results are descriptive and provisional. They are not advice about parties, candidates or votes.
